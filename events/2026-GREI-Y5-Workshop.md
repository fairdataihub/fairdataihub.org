## 📝 Event Description

The **Generalist Repository Ecosystem Initiative (GREI)** will host **The Evolving Data Sharing Ecosystem: Revisiting the Role of Generalist Repositories**, a virtual workshop held over two half-days. GREI is an NIH initiative that brings seven generalist repositories together to build consistent capabilities, services, metrics, and community infrastructure for sharing and reusing NIH-funded data. The workshop will build on the 2020 NIH workshop that led to GREI's formation and the 2023 GREI Workshop, bringing together researchers, repository leaders, librarians, data professionals, funders, and community organizations to look at how data sharing has progressed and what comes next.

Through panel discussions, lightning talks, and community conversations, the workshop will explore connecting research outputs across repositories, demonstrating the impact of shared data, preparing research outputs for an AI-enabled future, and clarifying the roles of generalist, disciplinary, and institutional repositories.

**Panel 2 — Measuring the Impact of Data Sharing** will take up a question that has moved to the center of the conversation. With data sharing now routinely required, the focus has shifted from whether researchers are sharing to whether it is making a difference. The panel will examine how the impact of shared data is measured and demonstrated, from citations, reuse, and reproducibility to the harder-to-count downstream effects on science and health. Panelists will discuss how open-metrics infrastructure is maturing, how impact can feed back into evaluation and credit, and what it will take to make data-sharing impact visible and rewarded for researchers.

Michael Chiang, Director of the National Eye Institute (NEI), will introduce the session. Each panelist will give a short presentation, followed by 30–40 minutes of moderated discussion and Q&A.

## 💡 Our Role / Contribution

**Bhavesh Patel** will join Panel 2 as an NIH S-index Challenge winner, bringing the perspective of developing tools to measure data-sharing impact. He will open with a short presentation drawing on his work on the **S-index** and **[Scholar Data](https://scholardata.io)**, then join the panel discussion on what meaningful data-sharing impact looks like beyond download counts, what an index like the S-index can and cannot capture, the risks of scoring data sharing, and the common traits of frequently reused datasets.
